About the project

A calculator and a unit converter that stay out of your way.

MetriFlow is a free web app that puts a full scientific calculator and a unit converter in one clean, fast place. It runs in your browser, works on phones and computers, and never sends what you type to anyone.

Why MetriFlow exists

Everyday calculating and converting is usually scattered. A phone calculator with a handful of keys here, a search result for converting units there, and websites full of adverts, pop-ups and slow pages in between. MetriFlow brings the two jobs together in one calm place.

It was created by Lakshya Singh as a practical project with three goals: be accurate, be fast, and respect your privacy. Every part is written from scratch in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so there are no heavy libraries, no trackers and nothing that waits on another website.

The name describes the idea. Metri is for measuring, and Flow is for how smoothly you move from one value, one unit, or one calculation to the next.

How it works

Using MetriFlow takes four simple steps.

  1. Pick a tool

    Choose Calculator or Unit Converter from the home page or the menu at the top.

  2. Enter a value

    Tap the keys or type with your keyboard. In the calculator, open brackets close automatically.

  3. See the answer

    A live preview appears as you type. Press equals to keep the result and add it to your history.

  4. Make it yours

    Press the theme button to cycle through the four themes. The choice is remembered on every page.

Under the hood: how a calculation is worked out

  1. 1. InputWhat you type or tap, for example 2+3×sin(30).
  2. 2. CleanOnly allowed characters pass. Anything else is rejected.
  3. 3. SplitThe text is split into numbers, operators and functions.
  4. 4. SolveBrackets, powers, multiply and divide, then add and subtract, in the right order.
  5. 5. CheckImpossible answers are caught, and the result is shown to 12 digits.

Why not use eval?

Many simple calculators hand your text to the browser to run as code, which is risky. MetriFlow has its own small maths parser that only understands numbers, operators and a fixed list of functions, so it cannot run anything else.

Order of operations

Brackets first, then powers, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Powers work right to left, so 2^3^2 is 512, and −2^2 is −4.

Angle units

Press the angle button to switch between degrees (DEG), radians (RAD) and gradians (GRAD). Common cases are exact, so sin(30°) is 0.5 and tan(90°) is reported as invalid.

Accuracy

Computers store decimals approximately. MetriFlow shows 12 significant digits, which removes tiny rounding noise, so 0.1 + 0.2 appears as 0.3.

How the converter works

Each unit is stored as a factor of a base unit, such as the metre for length. To convert, the value is changed to the base unit and then into the unit you chose. Temperature uses its own formulas. The converter is still being built.

How themes work

Each theme is a set of colour variables in one stylesheet. The theme button changes one setting on the page, and your choice is saved so every page opens in the same theme.

Security and privacy

No website can honestly promise to be impossible to hack, and anyone can read a site's front-end code. What MetriFlow does is leave very little for an attacker to work with.

Your calculations stay on your device. The only things saved in your browser are your theme choice and your calculator history, and you can clear the history at any time with the Clear button.

  • No code from text. Input is never run as code. Unknown words and symbols are rejected.
  • Strict content policy. Only files from this site can load, with no inline scripts and no outside connections.
  • Limits on input. Length, nesting depth and function sizes are capped, so odd input cannot freeze the page.
  • Safe display. Results and history are shown as plain text, never as page markup.
  • Frame protection. The site refuses to be shown inside another website's page.
  • No trackers or cookies. No analytics, no adverts, no third-party fonts.

Questions and answers

Is MetriFlow free?

Yes. The calculator and converter are free to use, with no account and no adverts. Supporting the project is completely optional.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No calculation leaves your device. Only your theme choice and calculator history are saved, and they stay in your own browser.

Why are answers shown with 12 digits?

Computers store decimal numbers approximately. Showing 12 significant digits hides tiny rounding errors, so everyday sums look the way you expect.

What do DEG, RAD and GRAD mean?

They are angle units: degrees (360 in a full circle), radians (2π in a full circle) and gradians (400 in a full circle). Press the angle button in the calculator to change it. DEG is the default.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and the calculator keys are sized for easy tapping.

How do I report a problem or suggest a feature?

Email the support address in the Contact section below. Clear, short descriptions help the most.
